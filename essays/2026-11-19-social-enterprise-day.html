Minor occasion · 19 November 2026

Social Enterprise Day

Social Enterprise Day falls on Thursday 19 November in 2026. It celebrates businesses established to serve a social or environmental purpose through their trading. That might sound abstract until you think of a community bakery, a recycling service or a business creating employment for people who find other doors closed. The occasion invites such organisations to explain what they do and why their business model matters. Social Enterprise UK’s current campaign page confirms today’s date and encourages participants to show the difference they make in their communities.

The date moves because the day is linked to the Thursday of Global Entrepreneurship Week. Social Enterprise UK’s 2018–19 impact report documents that relationship and describes its earlier Who Knew campaign, in which organisations shared photographs and stories about their work. That provides a useful piece of the day’s history without pretending to know an unverified founding year. The recurring purpose is clear: make work that can be overlooked more visible, and help people recognise the social enterprises already operating around them.

What makes a social enterprise distinctive is the place of its mission in the business. Social Enterprise UK describes organisations whose social or environmental purpose appears in their governing documents, which earn most of their income through trading and use at least half their profits or surpluses for that mission. Transparency also matters. These criteria make the idea more concrete than a general claim to do good. The purpose shapes how the business operates, who benefits and what happens to the money it earns.

Britain’s legal history supplies another useful strand. Community interest companies, usually shortened to CICs, were introduced in 2005 as a company form for community benefit. Companies House explains that they can undertake anything from small local projects to substantial health services. They can allow financial returns, but these must be balanced with their community purpose. A CIC is one possible form for a social enterprise; the terms are not interchangeable. The existence of the form shows how an ambition to combine trading and public benefit became part of company regulation.

The 2026 UK campaign brings the focus back to place. Social Enterprise UK asks organisations to share a graphic showing their community impact and to post together at 10am UK time on 19 November. Organisations working across several locations can highlight individual projects or sites. The collective action is simple, but its point is useful: connect a national movement with things people can recognise in their own neighbourhoods. Elsewhere, the global character of the day allows organisations to tell their own stories in locally appropriate ways.

For JT, this is a good day to look more closely at a service or shop that already seems part of everyday life. Who owns it, whom does it employ, and where do its surpluses go? Those questions can uncover a different account of a familiar place. Taking part need not mean attending a conference. Reading an organisation’s impact report, recommending its work or becoming a customer can be a more practical response. The day’s warmest promise is modest but meaningful: the ordinary business of making, selling and providing services can also give people a stronger stake in the place where they live.
