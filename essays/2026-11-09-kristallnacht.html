Major occasion · Monday 9 November 2026

Kristallnacht — remembering the Night of Broken Glass

Kristallnacht, the “Night of Broken Glass”, was a violent pogrom that took place on 9 and 10 November 1938 throughout Germany, which by then included Austria and the Sudetenland. Nazis burned more than 1,400 synagogues, vandalised thousands of Jewish-owned businesses, broke into homes and desecrated religious objects. About 26,000 Jewish men were imprisoned in concentration camps simply for being Jewish.

The regime used the shooting of the German diplomat Ernst vom Rath in Paris as its excuse. From 7 November, Propaganda Minister Goebbels coordinated the press response, blaming all Jews for the attack, and the Gestapo chief ordered the arrest of 20,000 to 30,000 Jewish men.

Today the date is a day of remembrance in Germany and beyond, with vigils, readings of names and ceremonies at rebuilt or surviving synagogues and memorials. The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum’s account is a good place to read more.
