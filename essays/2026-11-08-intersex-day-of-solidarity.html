Intersex Day of Solidarity — remembering a life, standing with others

Intersex Day of Solidarity, also called Intersex Day of Remembrance, is observed each year on 8 November. It marks the birthday of Herculine Barbin, a French intersex person whose memoirs were later published by the philosopher Michel Foucault, together with contemporary documents and a later fictionalised account. The day is a chance to remember one life that was long spoken about more than listened to, and to stand with intersex people whose rights and bodily autonomy are still contested.

The observance appears to have been held first on 8 November 2005, as Intersex Solidarity Day, after Joëlle-Circé Laramée, then the Canadian spokeswoman for Organisation Intersex International, called on anyone concerned with intersex human rights to mark the date. It is distinct from Intersex Awareness Day, which falls on 26 October. Today the two dates form a short season of attention: one for raising public understanding, and this one for remembrance and solidarity.
