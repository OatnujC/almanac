Major occasion · Monday 9 November 2026

Fall of the Berlin Wall — the night the border opened

On the evening of 9 November 1989, East German spokesman Günter Schabowski held a live press conference. He had been handed a note about new travel rules only shortly before and had not been fully briefed. His muddled answer about when the rules took effect was broadcast on East German television and radio, and crowds gathered at the crossings in Berlin that night.

The announcement came after months of pressure. Hungary had removed its border fence in May and opened its border in August, thousands of East Germans fled through embassies and neighbouring countries, and long-time leader Erich Honecker was ousted in October. By the end of the night the border had effectively lost its grip, and the Wall, built in 1961, was no longer a barrier.

Germany was formally reunified on 3 October 1990, now the country’s national day, so 9 November is remembered rather than celebrated as a public holiday. Each year Berlin marks it with ceremonies, memorial events and light displays along the former border line. The date is also heavy with other German history, including the Kristallnacht pogrom of 1938, which is why the two are often remembered together.
