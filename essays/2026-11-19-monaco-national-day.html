Minor occasion · 19 November 2026

Monaco National Day

Every year on 19 November the tiny principality of Monaco turns red and white. National Day, known locally as the Fête du Prince, or Prince’s Day, is the day Monégasques honour their ruling family and their country, with a church service, military honours, a parade, fireworks and plenty of food and music. It is a public holiday, and if it falls on a Sunday the following Monday is taken off instead.

A date chosen by the prince

Monaco’s National Day has an unusual rule: the date is chosen by the reigning prince, traditionally to match the feast day of his patron saint. That is why it has moved several times. According to Riviera Radio’s history of the tradition, it began in 1857, when Prince Charles III declared 4 November, the feast of his patron saint, to be the Sovereign’s Day. Monaco was then turning itself into a modern independent state, with its own institutions and diplomatic relations, and the celebration became part of that national identity.

When Charles III died in 1889, his successor Albert I moved the celebration to 15 November, Saint Albert’s day. Prince Louis II, who came to the throne in 1922, faced a practical problem: his own saint’s day fell on 25 August, in the middle of the summer holidays when many businesses were closed. He chose instead 17 January, the feast of Saint Anthony the Abbot, patron saint of his granddaughter Princess Antoinette. It was during his reign, in 1923, that the official gazette, the Journal de Monaco, began calling the occasion National Day.

Why 19 November

Prince Rainier III came to the throne in 1949. At first National Day was held on 11 April, the feast of Blessed Rainier. But in 1952 that date fell on Good Friday, so a Sovereign Ordinance of 27 October 1951 moved it permanently to 19 November. The new date had two attractions: it was Saint Rainier’s Day, and it recalled the celebrations of Rainier III’s accession on 19 November 1949.

When Rainier’s son, Prince Albert II, succeeded him in 2005, he could have returned the day to 15 November, Saint Albert’s day, as tradition would suggest. Instead he kept 19 November as a tribute to his father, and held his own investiture celebrations on that date in 2005. So Monaco has now celebrated National Day on the same date for more than seven decades.

How the day unfolds

Many of today’s rituals go back to the 19th century. The first celebrations included a Te Deum, a service of thanksgiving, at the church of Saint-Nicolas, before Monaco Cathedral was built. The Monégasque national anthem was played in church for the first time in 1863, and in 1871 the first military parade took place in Palace Square after the Te Deum. Fireworks, concerts and traditional games on the Rock followed.

Today the day still begins with a Te Deum at Monaco Cathedral, attended by the princely family. Then come military honours and a parade in Palace Square, in which the Prince’s Carabiniers and the firefighters have long taken part; a police detachment joined for the first time in 1952. Medals are presented, the streets are decorated in Monaco’s red and white, and there are concerts, performances and traditional Monégasque food. A funfair keeps going for a week after the day itself. One gentle custom is the distribution of gifts to older Monégasque citizens by members of the princely family.

This year, Riviera Radio reports, the festivities began on the evening of Wednesday 18 November with fireworks over Port Hercule at 8.10pm local time, ahead of the main ceremonies on Thursday 19 November. Around the occasion, Prince Albert II and Princess Charlene also hosted a reception at the Yacht Club de Monaco for nearly 400 members of the diplomatic and consular community. More than 160 years after it began, the Fête du Prince remains a celebration of the principality’s history and of the close bond between Monaco and its sovereign.
