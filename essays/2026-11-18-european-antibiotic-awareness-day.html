Minor occasion · 18 November 2026

European Antibiotic Awareness Day and World AMR Awareness Week

Today is European Antibiotic Awareness Day, and the first day of World AMR Awareness Week, which runs from 18 to 24 November. The European day, coordinated by the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control, has been kept on or around 18 November since 2008. The World Health Organization later built a global week around the same date. This year’s WHO theme is “One Health, One Action: Prevent AMR Now.” The European campaign’s own theme is “Care for your kids – use antibiotics wisely.”

What antimicrobial resistance is

Antimicrobial resistance, or AMR, happens when bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites change over time and no longer respond to the medicines meant to kill them. Infections become harder, and sometimes impossible, to treat. The problem is bigger than antibiotics alone, which is why the language has widened from “antibiotic resistance” to resistance among all antimicrobials, but antibiotics remain the part most of us meet in ordinary life. Every unnecessary course, every course stopped too early, and every dose used in animals or on farms when it is not needed, gives resistant organisms more chances to spread.

The human cost is already large. A widely cited analysis in The Lancet, covering the year 2019, estimated that 1.27 million deaths were directly attributable to bacterial antimicrobial resistance, and that nearly five million deaths were associated with drug-resistant bacterial infections. Those are not future projections. They are a baseline from which the world is trying, late, to climb down. A global action plan was endorsed by the World Health Assembly in 2015, and one of its first aims was simply to improve public and professional understanding of the problem.

How the day and the week grew

European Antibiotic Awareness Day came first, as a platform for national campaigns on using antibiotics only when they are needed, in people and in animals, in line with a “One Health” approach that treats human, animal and environmental health as connected. WHO launched a global World Antibiotic Awareness Week in 2015. In 2020 the name became World Antimicrobial Awareness Week, to cover antifungals, antivirals and antiparasitics as well as antibiotics. In 2023 it was renamed again, to World AMR Awareness Week, keeping the familiar initials WAAW. The dates, 18 to 24 November, have stayed put.

The 2026 messages

WHO’s 2026 theme stresses that preventing resistance is not a job for doctors alone. “One Health, One Action” is a call for coordinated work across human medicine, veterinary practice, agriculture and the environment, because resistant organisms do not respect those boundaries. The European campaign this year is more intimate. It focuses on children, especially infants and preschoolers, who are given antibiotics more often than almost any other group, even though many common childhood infections are viral and do not respond to antibiotics at all. The materials are aimed at parents and carers: how to manage everyday infections, when antibiotics are needed and when they are not, what the side effects can be, and when to seek medical advice. ECDC also said that fresh European data on resistance and on antibiotic use would be published on 18 November, and that the campaign would work with social media communicators in each EU and EEA country, in their own languages.

The practical advice has not really changed since 2008, and that is part of the point. Do not press for antibiotics if a qualified clinician says they will not help. If they are prescribed, take them as directed. Never share leftover pills or save them for next time. The medicines still work for millions of people. The day exists because that will not remain true automatically, and because the children at the centre of this year’s European campaign will need them to work when they are grown.
