Minor occasion · Saturday 14 November 2026

The King’s birthday

King Charles III turns 78 today. He was born at Buckingham Palace on the evening of 14 November 1948, the first child of Princess Elizabeth, then heir to the throne, and Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh. Because the King's real birthday falls in dark, damp November, the public celebration of it, with Trooping the Colour on Horse Guards Parade, takes place on an official birthday in June instead; in 2026 that was Saturday 13 June. But his actual birthday is still marked, quietly, around the country.

A designated flag day

The most visible sign is on government buildings. By command of the King, there is a short list of designated days on which the Union Flag must be flown on UK government buildings, and 14 November, the Birthday of His Majesty The King, is one of them. It sits on the 2026 list alongside days such as St George's Day, Coronation Day (6 May), Accession Day (8 September) and Remembrance Sunday (8 November). The Scottish Government follows the same date, and many councils and other organisations choose to follow suit.

A birth in post-war Britain

Charles was born into a country still under rationing, three years after the end of the Second World War. The news was announced by a notice pinned to the railings of Buckingham Palace, and crowds gathered outside. The fountains in Trafalgar Square were turned blue to mark the arrival of a boy. His grandfather, George VI, was on the throne; his mother would become Queen in February 1952, when he was three, and he became heir apparent at that moment. He was created Prince of Wales in 1958 and formally invested at Caernarfon Castle in 1969.

He waited longer than anyone in British history to take the throne. He became King on 8 September 2022 on the death of Queen Elizabeth II, at the age of 73, the oldest person to accede to the British throne, and was crowned with Queen Camilla at Westminster Abbey on 6 May 2023.

Notable birthdays

Some of his birthdays have been milestones. His 21st in 1969 came months after his investiture. For his 60th in 2008 a series of events celebrated his charitable work, and for his 70th in 2018 there were celebrations at Buckingham Palace and a well-known BBC documentary about his life at 70. His 75th birthday in 2023, his first as King, saw the launch of the Coronation Food Project, aimed at reducing food waste and getting surplus food to people in need, an initiative that reflected interests he had pursued for decades. In recent years, after his cancer diagnosis was announced in 2024, his birthdays have also been occasions on which well-wishers send messages of support.

A life of causes

Long before he was King, Charles was known for his campaigning on subjects that were sometimes unfashionable at the time: the environment and climate change, organic farming, architecture and traditional craft skills, and interfaith understanding. He founded The Prince's Trust in 1976 with his Royal Navy severance pay, and it has since helped well over a million young people into work, training and education; it is now known as The King's Trust. Many of his birthdays have been used to highlight those causes rather than for lavish celebration.

Sharing the day

Among the others born on 14 November are the Impressionist painter Claude Monet (1840), the Indian prime minister Jawaharlal Nehru (1889) and Frederick Banting, co-discoverer of insulin (1891), whose birthday is World Diabetes Day. So the King shares his date with an unusually distinguished crowd, and with a day dedicated to health awareness worldwide.
