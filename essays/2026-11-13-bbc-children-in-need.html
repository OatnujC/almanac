Major occasion · Friday 13 November 2026

BBC Children in Need Appeal Night

Tonight, Friday 13 November 2026, BBC One and iPlayer hand their whole evening over to BBC Children in Need. From 7pm the appeal show goes out live from Dock10 at MediaCityUK in Salford, hosted this year by Alex Jones, Mel Giedroyc, Rylan, Sara Cox and Tyler West, with three hours of sketches, music, celebrity surprises and the much-loved Children in Need Choir. Across the country, landmarks are lighting up yellow to mark the night, a glow-up that began earlier this week when BBC Broadcasting House in London turned yellow on The One Show.

Children in Need is the BBC’s only in-house charity. It raises money all year for projects that support disadvantaged children and young people across the United Kingdom, and the appeal night is the moment when all of that fundraising, from school bake sales and sponsored silences to office fancy dress, comes together on screen. The BBC says the need has never been greater: right now the charity can fund only about one in six of the organisations that ask it for help.

Why a Friday in November?

There is no fixed date in the calendar for Children in Need. The telethon is a television event, and the BBC usually schedules it on a Friday evening in mid-November, most often the second or third Friday of the month. Over the years it has landed anywhere from 13 to 27 November. This year the BBC chose Friday 13 November, which makes it one of the rare years, along with 2015 and 2020, when the appeal has fallen on a Friday the 13th. Pudsey Bear, with his famous bandana over one eye, has never seemed too troubled by superstition.

Where it came from

The BBC has been asking its audiences to help children for almost a century. Its very first charity appeal was a five-minute radio broadcast on Christmas Day 1927, which raised roughly £1,342 for four children’s charities. From 1955 there was a televised appeal for children as well, fronted for years by Harry Corbett and his glove puppet Sooty. These short broadcasts ran every year until 1979, raising a total of around £625,000. A young Irish broadcaster called Terry Wogan presented the five-minute appeal in 1978 and again in 1979.

The big change came in 1980. On 21 November that year the BBC staged its first dedicated Children in Need telethon, breaking into the evening’s normal programmes with fundraising appeals. Wogan presented it with Sue Lawley and Esther Rantzen, and it raised about £1.2 million, a huge leap on what the old appeals had brought in. By 1984 the telethon had become one continuous programme, and it grew into a marathon that could run for six or seven hours. Wogan stayed at its heart for 35 years, until ill health made him step down in 2014; he died in 2016. Since then the charity has awarded the Sir Terry Wogan Fundraiser of the Year in his memory.

The charity was registered in England and Wales in 1988 and in Scotland in 2008. According to the BBC, it has raised more than £1 billion since 1980.

Pudsey Bear

The charity’s mascot was born in 1985, when a BBC graphic designer called Joanna Lane was asked to refresh the logo. She hit on the idea of a teddy bear and named him after her home town of Pudsey in West Yorkshire, where her grandfather had been mayor. The first Pudsey was a sad-faced orange bear with a red bandana. A year later he was redrawn with a smile and a white bandana with red spots, and in 2007 the spots became multicoloured. When the BBC rebranded in 2022, Pudsey was taken out of the logo itself, but he is still the face of the appeal, with larger spots on his bandana and spotty soles on his feet. There is even a Pudsey Bear made of plants in Pudsey Park.

How the night works now

Since 2020 the show has been trimmed to three hours, from 7pm to 10pm, and since 2021 it has come from Salford rather than London. The broadcast pauses briefly for the news, and the BBC moves the television watershed back to 11.30pm on appeal night so that the evening stays family-friendly. Through the night, BBC regions and nations cut in with local updates, and one of the most watched moments is the live choir, introduced in 2011, in which more than a thousand children in different parts of the UK sing together at the same time.

Music has always been part of the appeal. Over the years official Children in Need singles have included Lou Reed’s all-star Perfect Day in 1997, S Club 7, Girls Aloud and Gareth Malone’s All Star Choir, several of which went to number one.

The appeal has not been without criticism. Commentators have questioned the efficiency of its grant-giving model and some of the groups it funds, and in 2024 its chair stepped down after a dispute over a grant. But for most people in Britain the night is a familiar, cheerful fixture of the late-autumn calendar.

The night is only part of the story. The amount announced on screen is a running total, and money keeps coming in for weeks afterwards. Last year’s appeal, on 14 November 2025, had raised £45.5 million by the end of the broadcast. If you want to join in tonight, the show starts at 7pm, and the charity’s website explains how to give.
