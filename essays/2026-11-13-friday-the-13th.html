Minor occasion · Friday 13 November 2026

Friday the 13th

If you feel a little uneasy today, you are in large company. Friday the 13th is the best-known unlucky day in Western superstition, and 2026 has been an unusually busy year for it. Today is the third Friday the 13th this year, after February and March. That is the most any calendar year can have, and it only happens in two kinds of year. 2026 is a common year that began on a Thursday, which puts Friday the 13ths in February, March and November. The next year to do the same will be 2037.

How the calendar makes it

A Friday the 13th happens whenever a month begins on a Sunday, and every year has at least one. On average one comes round every 212 days or so. The longest possible gap between two of them is 14 months, and the shortest is a single month, exactly what happened between February and March this year. Curiously, because the Gregorian calendar repeats exactly every 400 years, the 13th of the month is very slightly more likely to land on a Friday than on any other day of the week: 688 times in each 400-year cycle, against 684 to 687 for the other days.

Where the superstition comes from

The fear really joins two older superstitions. The number 13 had long been thought unlucky. One story often cited is a Norse myth in which twelve gods were feasting in Valhalla when the trickster Loki arrived uninvited as the thirteenth guest and arranged for the blind god Höðr to kill Balder, the god of joy, with a mistletoe arrow. In Christian tradition, there were thirteen people at the Last Supper, on the night before the crucifixion on Good Friday.

Friday, separately, was thought to be an unlucky day to start a journey or a new project at least as far back as Chaucer in the 14th century. But historians have found no record of the two being put together as an especially unlucky combination before the 19th century. A popular claim ties it to the arrest of the Knights Templar on Friday 13 October 1307, but the link is not supported by evidence, and the real origins remain murky.

The earliest clear sign is French. A character in an 1834 play complains that he was born on Friday 13 December 1813, and that all his misfortunes stem from it. One of the earliest references in English concerns the composer Gioachino Rossini, who died on Friday 13 November 1868, exactly 158 years ago today. His biographer noted that Rossini, like many Italians, thought Fridays and the number 13 unlucky, and found it remarkable that he died on a day that combined both. The superstition really caught on in the English-speaking world after 1907, when Thomas W. Lawson’s bestselling novel Friday, the Thirteenth told of a broker who exploits it to cause a panic on Wall Street. The horror films that began in 1980 sealed its reputation.

Is it actually unlucky?

The fear even has a name, paraskevidekatriaphobia, and one American estimate suggests it affects millions of people. A 1993 study in the British Medical Journal of traffic on the M25 famously found fewer cars but more hospital admissions on Friday the 13th, though its authors admitted the numbers were too small to mean much, and later studies found no real link. In 2008 Dutch insurers reported slightly fewer accidents on Friday the 13th than on other Fridays, perhaps because people are more careful.

Other unlucky days

Not everyone fears this date. In Spanish-speaking countries and in Greece, the unlucky day is Tuesday the 13th. Greeks link Tuesday with Ares, the god of war, and note that Constantinople fell on a Tuesday in both 1204 and 1453. In Italy, the dreaded date is Friday the 17th, partly because the Roman numerals XVII can be rearranged to spell VIXI, Latin for “I have lived”, in other words, “I am dead”. Thirteen is often seen as lucky there.

Some people have turned the day around. Finland holds its National Accident Day, a safety awareness campaign, on Friday the 13th, and tattoo parlours in many countries run special Friday the 13th sales of designs featuring the number 13, which some wearers claim keeps bad luck away. And in Britain this year, the date belongs to a very good cause: Children in Need is on tonight.
