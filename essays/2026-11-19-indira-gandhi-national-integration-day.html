Minor occasion · 19 November 2026

Indira Gandhi’s Birth Anniversary and National Integration Day

On 19 November India remembers Indira Gandhi, its first and so far only woman prime minister, who was born on this day in 1917. Today is her 109th birth anniversary. The date is also observed as National Integration Day, known in Hindi and Urdu as Qaumi Ekta Diwas, and it opens Qaumi Ekta Week, seven days of events across the country devoted to communal harmony and national unity.

Who she was

Indira Priyadarshini Nehru was born in Allahabad, now Prayagraj, into a Kashmiri Pandit family. She was the daughter of Jawaharlal Nehru, India’s first prime minister, and Kamala Nehru. During her father’s premiership from 1947 to 1964 she acted as his hostess and travelled with him abroad. In 1942 she married Feroze Gandhi, later a member of parliament, and they had two sons, Rajiv and Sanjay. She entered the upper house of parliament, the Rajya Sabha, in 1964 and served as minister of information and broadcasting under Lal Bahadur Shastri. After Shastri’s death in January 1966 she became prime minister, the world’s second woman to lead a government after Sirimavo Bandaranaike of Sri Lanka.

She held the office from 1966 to 1977 and again from 1980 until 1984. Her time in power was dramatic and remains much debated. Her government nationalised the banks and abolished the privy purses paid to former princely rulers. In 1971 she led India in war with Pakistan in support of the independence movement in East Pakistan, a conflict that ended with the creation of Bangladesh. India carried out its first nuclear test in 1974. Her supporters also point to the Green Revolution in agriculture and her anti-poverty campaigns, which earned her the name Mother Indira among many poor and rural Indians. Her critics point above all to the Emergency of 1975 to 1977, when she ruled by decree, civil liberties were suspended and more than 100,000 opponents, journalists and dissenters were imprisoned. Her party lost the 1977 election, but she returned to power in a landslide in 1980.

In 1984 she ordered Operation Blue Star, a military action at the Golden Temple in Amritsar that killed hundreds of Sikhs. On 31 October 1984 she was assassinated by two of her Sikh bodyguards, and her death was followed by anti-Sikh riots in Delhi. Her total time in office, 15 years and 350 days, makes her India’s second-longest-serving prime minister after her father. She was cremated in Delhi at the site now called Shakti Sthal, “place of strength”, marked by a great rock of iron ore that commemorates her as the “Iron Lady of India”. She was awarded the Bharat Ratna, India’s highest civilian honour, in 1971, and in 1999 she was named “Woman of the Millennium” in a BBC online poll.

A week for unity

National Integration Day falls on her birthday, and it is the first day of Qaumi Ekta Week, which runs from 19 to 25 November. According to India’s Ministry of Home Affairs, the aim of the week is to foster communal harmony, national integration and pride in the country’s composite culture. Each day has its own theme. 19 November is National Integration Day, with meetings, symposia and seminars on secularism, anti-communalism and non-violence. It is followed by Welfare of Minorities Day on 20 November, Linguistic Harmony Day on the 21st, with literary events and poetry gatherings to help people appreciate other regions’ languages, Weaker Sections Day on the 22nd, Cultural Unity Day on the 23rd, a Women’s Day on the 24th and Conservation Day on the 25th. The National Foundation for Communal Harmony, an autonomous body under the ministry, runs a Communal Harmony Campaign alongside the week and marks Communal Harmony Flag Day on 25 November.

A central part of the day is the National Integration Pledge, taken by government employees and students. Its words, as reported by The Sentinel of Assam, include the promise: “I solemnly pledge to work with dedication to preserve and strengthen the freedom and integrity of the nation. I further affirm that I shall never resort to violence and that all differences and disputes relating to religion, language, region or other political or economic grievances should be settled by peaceful and constitutional means.” The day also draws attention to Article 51A(e) of India’s constitution, which asks every citizen to promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood.

How the day is marked

Government offices, schools and colleges hold seminars, cultural programmes and awareness campaigns during the week. Political leaders pay tribute to Indira Gandhi on her birthday; this morning Prime Minister Narendra Modi posted on X: “Paying tribute to former Prime Minister Smt. Indira Gandhi Ji on her birth anniversary.” More than four decades after her death, she remains, as one Indian newspaper put it today, a defining figure in the country’s political history.
