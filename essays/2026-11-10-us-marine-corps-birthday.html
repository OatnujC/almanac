US Marine Corps Birthday

Today is the birthday of the United States Marine Corps, celebrated by Marines around the world every 10 November. This year the Corps turns 251. Wherever Marines are serving, whether at a base in North Carolina, on a ship at sea or at an embassy far from home, the day is marked with the same rituals: a message from the Commandant, a formal ball and a cake cut with a sword.

Why 10 November

On 10 November 1775, early in the American War of Independence, the Second Continental Congress, meeting in Philadelphia, passed a resolution to raise two battalions of Marines. The resolution asked that "particular care be taken" to enlist only men who were good seamen or familiar with maritime affairs, since they would serve aboard ships and fight at sea and ashore. Those first units were known as the Continental Marines.

Tradition holds that the first recruits signed up at Tun Tavern, a Philadelphia inn, under Samuel Nicholas, who is regarded as the first Commandant. Historians have debated whether recruiting actually began earlier at Nicholas's family tavern, the Conestoga Waggon, but Tun Tavern has become the Corps' sentimental birthplace.

A birthday rediscovered

The story has a curious twist. When the war ended in 1783, the Continental Navy and the Continental Marines were disbanded. The Marine Corps as an ongoing body was re-established by an act signed by President John Adams on 11 July 1798, and for over a century Marines quietly marked that July date.

That changed in 1921. Major Edwin North McClellan, head of the Corps' small historical section, suggested to the Commandant, Major General John A. Lejeune, that the original date of 10 November should become a Corps-wide holiday. Lejeune agreed and issued Marine Corps Order No. 47, which recalled the history and spirit of the Corps and directed that it be read to every command each 10 November. It is still read aloud today, alongside a new birthday message from the current Commandant.

The ball and the cake

The first formal birthday ball was held in 1925. Early celebrations varied widely, with dances, mock battles, pageants and sporting events, but over the decades a set ceremony took shape. At a Marine Corps birthday ball, a cake is wheeled in and cut with a Mameluke sword, the curved ceremonial sword carried by Marine officers. The first piece traditionally goes to the guest of honour, and the next to the oldest Marine present, who passes it to the youngest, a small, touching gesture of experience handing tradition down to a new generation.

Marines are famously serious about this day. Small detachments in remote places hold their own celebrations, and veterans gather for reunions and dinners. Many Marines simply greet each other with "Happy birthday, Marine".

Soldiers of the sea

The idea of marines, soldiers who serve at sea and fight from ships, is much older than the American Corps. Britain's Royal Marines trace their origins to 28 October 1664, when a regiment for sea service was raised under King Charles II, and the Continental Marines were partly modelled on British marines of the day. The two corps have trained and served alongside each other for generations.

A neighbourly coincidence

The Marine Corps birthday falls the day before Veterans Day in the United States, which shares its date, 11 November, with Armistice Day here. So for many American Marines, these two days run together as a time to celebrate the Corps and to honour all who have served.
