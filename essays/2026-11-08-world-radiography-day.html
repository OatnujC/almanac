Minor occasion · Sunday 8 November 2026

World Radiography Day — when unseen light changed medicine

On 8 November 1895, working late in his laboratory at the Physical Institute in Würzburg, Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen noticed a previously unknown kind of radiation. It passed through materials that ordinary light could not, and he called it X-rays, the X standing for the unknown. The discovery was almost immediately useful: for the first time, physicians could look inside a living body without cutting it open. Röntgen refused to patent the finding, believing it should benefit everyone. In 1901 he received the first Nobel Prize in Physics, and he gave the prize money to the University of Würzburg.

A common retelling places the famous image of his wife Bertha’s hand, complete with her ring, on that same November night. The Röntgen Memorial in Würzburg dates that radiograph to December 1895. He presented the discovery in public on 23 January 1896, to the Physical-Medical Society of Würzburg, and during the lecture he X-rayed the hand of the anatomist Albert von Kölliker, who proposed that the new rays be called Röntgen rays.

The anniversary is marked each year as World Radiography Day, a thank-you to radiographers and support workers and a chance to explain the place of diagnostic imaging and radiotherapy in modern care. The same date is also the International Day of Radiology, which honours radiologists, radiographers and other imaging professionals and draws attention to how unevenly access to those services remains around the world. In 2026 the UK Society of Radiographers’ theme is professionalism in radiography.
