Minor occasion · Sunday 8 November 2026

Synaxis of the Archangel Michael — a gathering of the heavenly host

On 8 November many Orthodox churches keep the Synaxis of the Archangel Michael and the Other Bodiless Powers: a feast that honours Michael, Gabriel, Raphael and the wider company of angels. A synaxis is a gathering. The day is not only Michael’s, though he is named as chief of the heavenly hosts; it is a single commemoration of the angelic world as the Church understands it.

According to Orthodox tradition recorded by the Orthodox Church in America, the feast was established early in the fourth century in connection with the Council of Laodicea, which met a few years before the First Ecumenical Council. The council’s 35th canon condemned the worship of angels as gods and rulers of the world, while affirming their proper veneration. Tradition also explains the date symbolically: November was the ninth month when the year was counted from March, matching nine ranks of angels, and the eighth day of the month was chosen because the “Eighth Day” is a name the Fathers gave to the Last Judgment, when the Son of Man comes in glory and all the holy angels with him.

The civil date depends on which calendar a church follows. Churches that use the revised or new calendar, including the Orthodox Church in America, mark the synaxis on 8 November 2026. Churches that still follow the Julian calendar keep the feast on 8 November of that calendar, which falls thirteen days later on the civil calendar, on 21 November 2026.
