Minor occasion · Sunday 8 November 2026

World Town Planning Day — how places become liveable

World Town Planning Day, also called World Urbanism Day, is kept each year on 8 November. It asks a simple, practical question: how should towns and cities be arranged so that people can live, work and gather in them well? Streets, homes, parks, transport and the less visible systems beneath them are easy to take for granted until they fail. This is a day for noticing the craft that tries to hold those things together.

The observance began in 1949, when Carlos María della Paolera, a professor at the University of Buenos Aires, started it to raise public and professional interest in planning, at home and abroad. The International Society of City and Regional Planners notes that it is now marked in some thirty countries across four continents. In 2026 the day reaches its 77th year. Planners and communities mark it with lectures, school competitions, awards and local events. The Royal Town Planning Institute, joining a wider Global Planners Network campaign, describes planning as work that is strongest when it draws on local knowledge and lived experience as well as professional skill, especially as communities face housing pressures, climate change and rapid technological change.
