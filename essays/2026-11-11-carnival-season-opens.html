Minor occasion · Wednesday 11 November 2026

Carnival season opens at 11:11 (Germany)

At precisely 11:11 this morning, while Britain falls silent, tens of thousands of people in Cologne will be doing something very different: cheering, singing and drinking in fancy dress. In the Rhineland and other Catholic parts of Germany, 11 November is the opening of the carnival season, known as Karneval in Cologne, Fasching in Bavaria and Fastnacht in Mainz and the south-west. Locals call it the "fifth season" of the year.

Why 11.11 at 11:11?

Carnival is the season of merrymaking before Lent, the forty days of fasting before Easter, so the real climax comes in late winter. In 2027 the street carnival peaks from Weiberfastnacht, the Thursday before Lent, through Rose Monday on 8 February, ending on Ash Wednesday, 10 February. So why start in November?

The number eleven is the key. In carnival tradition it is the "fools' number". Popular explanations vary: it is one more than the Ten Commandments and one short of the twelve apostles, so it stands for something slightly out of order; and in the Rhineland, which was occupied by revolutionary France in the 1790s, people like to say the letters E-L-F (German for eleven) stand for égalité, liberté, fraternité, the French revolutionary slogan, which carnivalists poked fun at. Cologne's carnival committee itself notes that this explanation is not fully settled. Whatever its origins, carnival societies are run by an "Elferrat", a Council of Eleven, a sort of mock parliament of the realm of fools, and the eleventh minute of the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month was the obvious moment to begin.

The date also has deeper roots. In the medieval church, St Martin's Day was the last feast before a forty-day fast leading up to Christmas, so 11 November was already a day for a final blow-out before belt-tightening, exactly the same logic as carnival before Lent.

The modern carnival is younger than it looks

People in the Rhineland have celebrated carnival for centuries, but the modern organised version dates back to Cologne in 1823, when a group of citizens founded a festival committee to tame the rowdy street celebrations into something more orderly, with costumed societies, a grand parade and a mock "Prince Carnival". It was during this reform that the Council of Eleven took shape, and the formal opening on 11 November followed later as the tradition developed.

How it is marked today

In Cologne the official opening takes place at 11:11 in the old town, at the Heumarkt square, with music, speeches and the presentation of the new carnival trio: the Prince, the Peasant and the Maiden (the Maiden is traditionally played by a man). The bars of the Altstadt and the student quarter fill with people dressed as clowns, animals and superheroes, and the city puts on extra police and transport to cope with the crowds.

In Düsseldorf, the rival carnival city just down the Rhine, the season begins with the "Hoppeditz-Erwachen", when the jester figure Hoppeditz wakes up at 11:11 and delivers a satirical speech. In Mainz, another stronghold, crowds gather to hear the carnival rules read out. All of this is followed by a strange pause: after the 11.11 kickoff, the parties largely go quiet through Advent and Christmas, and carnival proper resumes in January with costume balls and comic sessions before the big parades.

So today is a little like a trailer for the main event: one day of high spirits to say that the season of the fools is open, before everyone goes back to work and waits for February.
